# Steering, avoidance and path following

Navigation's corridor answers *where movement can go*. A locomotion controller answers *how this body moves now*. The desired velocity may combine seek/arrive toward the next corner, separation from neighbors and a local collision-avoidance correction, then pass through the ordinary character movement and collision code. Arrival needs a radius and speed threshold; otherwise agents oscillate around a point. Keep turning, acceleration and animation intent within the body controller.

**VERIFIED:** [DetourCrowd](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Include/DetourCrowd.h) manages corridor following and avoidance for agents on a navmesh. [Godot's avoidance documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.0/tutorials/navigation/navigation_using_navigationagents.html) explicitly warns that avoidance is separate from mesh/physics and can get agents stuck in constrained geometry. The installed Natural Selection 2 `BotMotion.lua` caches full paths, uses short local path queries and contains stuck detection. This indicates that path repair and stuck recovery deserve dedicated instrumentation.

**Proposed progression:** first follow corners with collision and a timeout; then add direct line shortcut checks; then bounded separation for crowded cases. Only adopt crowd simulation after measuring simultaneous agents and corridor conflicts on Android. Avoid requerying a full path every movement tick. Repath on blocked link, moved goal, stuck timer or significant nav revision. Narrow doors need a queue or temporary passage reservation; pure velocity avoidance cannot solve every head-on deadlock. Flying and climbing need separate movement modes and annotated links, not a ground mesh pretending to cover 3D space.
